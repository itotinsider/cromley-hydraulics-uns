# Business model

CH must learn to sell outcomes, not just equipment. The data in this environment is what makes
those offers possible. Real hydro OEMs already do this, which gives the case study credible
anchors.

| OEM | Offering | Signal for CH |

| [Andritz Hydro](https://www.andritz.com/hydro-en/hydronews/hn33/intelligent-monitoring) | Metris DiOMera monitoring; long-term O&M with KPI guarantees (Cerro del Águila, Peru) | Outcome-based contracts are possible in hydro |

## Possible CH tiers

1. **CH Connect** (bundled with new units): remote visibility, digital nameplate (`_meta/Nameplate`),
   data export.
2. **CH Monitor** (subscription per unit): alarms (`Events/Alarm`), monthly health report, expert
   review.
3. **CH Predict** (subscription + expert retainer): vibration analytics on the waveforms, residual
   models, remaining useful life, spare-parts forecast.
4. **CH Assure** (outcome-based): availability or rough-zone KPIs with bonus/malus, on-site smart
   spares, field-service SLAs.

## Discussion themes

- **Data ownership:** under the EU Data Act, users of connected products have data access rights
  (since 12 September 2025), and products placed on the market after 12 September 2026 must offer
  access by design. CH may only use non-personal product data under an agreement with the customer.
- **Third-party data sales:** only with customer consent; anonymised fleet benchmarks are more
  realistic than raw data.
- **Servitization paradox:** service investments often raise costs before returns arrive.
- **Outcome risk:** CH's KPIs depend on how the customer operates. In scenario C the dispatcher
  parks a unit in the rough zone: who pays for the bearing and runner life it costs?
- **Culture:** an engineering-first company must learn service delivery, and sales incentives tied
  to equipment sales must change.
